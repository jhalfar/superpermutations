# n = 7: what I tried

Started after Stefan Djokovic suggested a public "tried X, didn't work" list. Everything below is from my own notes, 6 Oct to 25 Nov 2026. Numbers are as measured, with the settings that matter.

## Where things stand

5,899 <= L(7) <= 5,905.

| bound | status |
|---|---|
| 5,898 | proved (Lebar's Lean proof with `native_decide`). I also built and checked a version without `native_decide`. |
| 5,899 | proved in Lean with 12 `native_decide` steps (Almanac, 9 Nov). A version without `native_decide` has 595,177 lemmas and took about 2,600 CPU hours. |
| 5,900 | proved on paper, with two independent programs per statement. The Lean version has 89 `native_decide` steps. |
| 5,901 | computed, not in Lean. All searches finished with no object. Mostly one program per statement. 7 of the 30 statements are in Lean. |
| 5,902 | open |
| 5,905 | upper bound, Teodorescu. His 4 words are 2 up to symmetry. Up to relabelling and reversal there are 98 words of 5,905 in total, 96 of them mine. |

I know no word of 5,904 or fewer.

## How to read the tables

- **Outcome** is one of four words. *Proved*: a written argument or a Lean proof, as the note says. *Computed negative*: a finished run or a measurement that gave no gain, inside the family and caps named in the row. *Inconclusive*: stopped, hit a limit, or not decided either way. *False*: a counterexample exists.
- "Negative" never means the idea is dead in general, and it never means a bound is false. Where it sits next to a speed-up, it means too slow for the budget named. I did not rerun any negative with 4x larger settings.
- Model. I describe a word as a kernel plus hanging components (checked on 26 words of n = 4 to 8). The defect D of a word of length L is L - 5,884, so L(7) = 5,884 + the least defect, and ruling out defect 14 gives 5,899. A kernel is a table of rows; a chain is a run of consecutive rows; a marker is a special position in a chain. "Without `native_decide`" means the Lean kernel checks every step itself, which is far slower.
- Exhaustive searches in section 2 used one implementation unless I say otherwise. Two items (the fixed-cut families and the Gurobi run, 2.12 and 2.13) came from a later pass and hold only under the restrictions named there.
